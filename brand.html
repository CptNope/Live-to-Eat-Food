Three floors, in this order, everywhere.

Worcester is a city of triple-deckers: stacked flats, painted siding, white window trim, a number by the door. The brand uses that building as its grammar, not as an illustration.

3

Top floor is Tina's take

Her opinion always sits highest: the note, the verdict, the story. An ad never occupies the top floor.

2

Middle floor is the food

Photography, dishes, what she ordered.

1

Street level is the door

Address, hours, directions, menu, reserve. The thing you act on is always at street level.

Logo

The name stacked in three floors between a heavy cornice and foundation rule. Archivo at weight 900, width 125, outlined.

  • Clear space is one floor height on every side. Minimum width 64px for the stacked mark.
  • Never recolor one floor, add a roof, fork or pin, or set the mark on a photo.

Color is painted siding

Used at page scale: one field color owns each region. Several siding colors appear together only in a row of houses.

  • Mustard is the front door: home hero, the Sunday list, Shrewsbury Street. Text on it is always asphalt.
  • Tina red fails contrast on every siding color, so her notes always sit on trim paper. Never use it for a link, button, sale or ad.
  • Secondary text: ink-2 #4A4C45 (8.0:1 on trim). Dashed not-yet outlines: ink-3 #6B6D64.
  • Faded siding for towns outside Worcester: blue-lt #B9CCD8, green-lt #C4CFB3, brick-lt #E8C4B4, asphalt text only.
  • Focus ring: 3px asphalt outline at 2px offset plus a 5px trim halo, so it reads on every field.

  • The note: a short line in her own words, ideally lifted from her post or video caption and linked to it. One per view, top floor only, always on trim paper, always also real text.
  • Verdicts replace stars. Words or nothing, taken from what she actually said; "Good, not the one" is set in ink-2, not red.

  • Shoot the dish in its place, with one clue about the room. No filters, no brand-color grading, no text on food photos except Tina's paper note.
  • Placeholders are honest: a field in the dish's own color, a dashed frame, the ratio and a one-line shot brief. No stock photos of other restaurants, no generated images of Tina.
  • Alt text names dish and place: "Neapolitan pizza at Volturno, 72 Shrewsbury St."

  • Specific beats enthusiastic: "Order the fried goat cheese" beats "Amazing tapas!"
  • Say where: every place is named with its street number.
  • First person belongs to Tina. The interface says "you" and "Tina".
  • No "foodie paradise", "culinary journey" or "drool-worthy". No exclamation marks in the interface.

Layout, motion, access

  • Fields, not cards: full-bleed painted sections separated by color. Rhythm: mustard, trim, asphalt, trim, blue, trim, mustard, brick, trim, asphalt.
  • Square corners everywhere. Only plates (3px) and the play button and route dots (round) are rounded. Rules, not shadows.
  • A row of places is a street: houses of different widths and heights on one curb, ending in an empty lot that asks for suggestions.
  • One authored motion moment per screen (save, video menu following the playhead, slot change). Nothing hidden at rest; reduced motion removes all transitions.
  • WCAG 2.1 AA, 44px touch targets (tab bar and door bar 64px), real buttons and links, visible focus.
  • On phones, the tab bar (Discover, Map, Saved, Tina) becomes the door bar on place pages: Directions, Call, Menu, Save.
